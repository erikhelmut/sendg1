Do tactile sensors on the feet of the Unitree G1 measurably improve locomotion
policies?

Every result here is a paired comparison: a baseline policy and a
tactile-augmented policy, same task, same seeds, same evaluation protocol. The
unit of work is **(task x observation-set x seed)**.

Built on [mjlab](https://github.com/mujocolab/mjlab), pinned to **1.6.0**
(commit `0fb8a681`).

## Status

- **Velocity tracking**, flat and rough: blind baselines; the tactile sole is
  modelled on the slipper's real 26-taxel layout, still idealised (no noise,
  latency or calibration error), so it is diagnostic for now.
- **Beam walking**: a blind G1 on a beam it cannot see, feeling it underfoot.
- **Box pushing** under unknown floor friction.

Tasks still on their own branches (blind vs touch actor, otherwise identical):

- **Pedalo** (`pedalo`): the G1 rides a Pedalo Classic or Sport at a joystick
  speed, or holds it still. Sport blind and touch are trained (one seed); both
  ride, with no clear difference yet.
- **Rolling cylinder** (`rolling-cylinder-balance`): stand across a free
  30 cm cylinder for 20 s without letting it roll away.
- **Slackline** (`slackline`): tandem stance on a tensioned line, walking
  along it on command. Untrained.
