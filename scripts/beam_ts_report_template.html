Walking a beam blind: a privileged teacher and a deployable student

A PPO teacher that knows where the beam is, distilled with DAgger into a GRU student that only uses what the real robot measures: IMU, joint encoders, its last action, the forward-speed command, and the tactile slipper under each foot.

The task

Seven beam widths, one straight beam

Each tile has a 1 m start platform, a 4 m straight beam and a 1 m end platform, standing on flat ground 0.15–0.30 m below (one height per terrain column). The command is a forward speed between 0 and 0.4 m/s; lateral and yaw commands are fixed at zero. When both feet and the base reach the end platform the command drops to zero, so the robot stops instead of walking off the far end. The ruler below draws every curriculum width to scale against the G1 sole (76 mm wide).

Setup

Who sees what

One environment computes two observation groups. real holds what the robot can measure; each term was checked against the unitree_sdk2 low-level state (LowState_.imu_state, motor_state[].q/dq), which has no base linear velocity, no contact and no terrain information. privileged holds simulator ground truth. The student's registration fails if its group contains any term not tagged onboard or sensor, and a test checks that guard with deliberate leaks.

Teacher (PPO)

Actor MLP 512-256-128, ELU, Gaussian with one scalar std; critic the same MLP. Both read real + privileged (257 dims), with running normalisation. 4096 envs, 24-step rollouts, 5 epochs × 4 minibatches, lr 1e-3 adaptive (KL 0.01), γ 0.99, λ 0.95, entropy 0.005.

Student (DAgger)

GRU(256) → MLP 256-128 over real only (160 dims). The GRU is the history: 50-step rollouts (1 s) and truncated BPTT over 25 steps (0.5 s). MSE onto the frozen teacher's mean action at every visited state. For the first 150 updates the teacher drives a linearly shrinking share of episodes (β 1 → 0, drawn per episode); after that the student drives alone.

Tactile features

The taxel pipeline is reused unchanged: per-contact forces give net force and CoP, four rays per taxel give which patches have support, and a Winkler plane splits the normal force over the 26 supported taxels. On top of that, each foot gets 33 numbers (66 for both). The right foot is re-indexed into the left foot's mirror image, so +y means lateral on both feet and the same weights serve both. Shear is left out: in this model it is the net tangential force split in proportion to load, so it adds no spatial information, and it is the channel least likely to transfer to the real sensor.

Reward

Final reward terms

This starts from the walking reward, keeping its regularisers. Foot-clearance and swing-height terms are dropped, because their raycasts hit the ground beside a narrow beam. Air time is relaxed. Lateral and yaw velocity tracking are gone with their commands. Weights are multiplied by the 0.02 s step.

Curriculum

Per-env width levels

LevelWidthSpawnCentreline widthPushesTactile fault strength
  • Promote one level when the episode ends without an early termination and the robot either reached the end platform or covered at least 70% of the commanded distance. A commanded distance under 0.5 m counts as standing and only has to survive.
  • Demote one level on any early termination. A time-out without tracking keeps the level.
  • Everyone starts at 0.40 m. Spawns move to the start platform from 0.20 m, because the default stance is 0.31 m wide at the outer sole edges and would not fit.
  • The share of standing commands falls from 60% to 15% as the mean level rises. Pushes start at 0.15 m and grow to ±0.3 m/s at the narrowest width.
  • The student starts spread uniformly over the levels the teacher masters, then follows the same rules on its own performance.

Domain randomisation: foot–terrain friction 0.4–1.2 (the feet carry contact priority, so this is the beam's friction as the robot feels it), contact softness (solref time constant 0.02–0.035 s), torso payload −1 to +3 kg, torso CoM ±2.5 cm, PD gains ±10%, effort limits 85–100%, actuation latency 0–20 ms, encoder bias ±0.015 rad, IMU noise plus a per-episode bias (gyro ±0.05 rad/s, gravity ±0.03), beam height 0.15–0.30 m, and the tactile faults above.

Next

Next steps

  • Curved and bent beams. The beam is already a centreline polyline that every reward, termination and observation projects onto. Adding curves means a new path generator in BeamTileCfg.beam_polyline and segment geometry, plus a yaw-rate command or a heading-free reward.
  • Tactile model fidelity. The sensor model is still built from the simulator's net force and CoP. Before deployment, calibrate gain, noise, saturation, latency and crosstalk against the real slipper, and replay recorded real readings through the feature code to check the feature ranges match.
  • Sim-to-real risks. The rigid beam and the 7-capsule foot make edge contacts sharper than a real rubber sole on wood. The IMU-only state means slow yaw drift goes unobserved without tactile cues. Payload and latency ranges are guesses; motor heating and battery sag are not modelled.
  • Ablations. Run the student without tactile (proprioception only) and with the 156-dim raw taxel array, to measure what the features contribute. Run 3–5 seeds per arm before making any claim.
  • Student fine-tuning. A short PPO phase on the student (with the privileged critic) may close part of the remaining gap at the narrowest widths.
