Walking a beam by touch: a teacher that only knows what its soles feel, and two students

A PPO teacher that sees ground truth crosses a 50 mm beam, but it balances on its centre of mass relative to the beam centreline, which nothing on the robot measures, so no student could copy it. A second teacher learns the beam only through what its tactile soles feel. Two GRU students are distilled from it with DAgger, one with the tactile features and one blind (proprioception only).

The task

Seven beam widths, one straight beam

Each tile has a 1 m start platform, a 4 m straight beam and a 1 m end platform, standing on flat ground 0.15–0.30 m below. The command is a forward speed between 0 and 0.4 m/s; lateral and yaw commands are fixed at zero. When both feet and the base reach the end platform the command drops to zero. The beam is a centreline polyline that every reward, termination and observation projects onto, so curved beams only need a new path generator. The ruler draws every curriculum width to scale against the G1 sole (76 mm wide).

How we got here

Why there are two teachers

  1. Privileged teacher (PPO).

    Sees the beam pose, feet and CoM offsets, true velocity and contact forces. After gait shaping (passing steps, flat feet, gentle touchdowns, symmetry augmentation) it crosses every width, including 50 mm, in ≥ 94% of runs.

  2. First students distilled from it: 0% success, three attempts.

    Two fixable causes and one that is not fixable. (1) Too few gradient steps and an MSE dominated by the wrists (action scale 0.075): fixed with a loss in joint-target space and 20 steps per iteration. (2) Copycat: with the previous action as input, a student learns "repeat the last action". While the teacher drives that input is the teacher's own action (error 0.004 with it, 0.34 with the student's own), and the student falls as soon as it drives. The previous action was removed from the students. (3) Information gap: a diagnostic student given the privileged inputs imitates almost perfectly (loss 0.003) and keeps succeeding once it drives, so the training loop works. The teacher's actions depend most on its CoM offset from the centreline and on the feet's lateral offsets (sensitivity chart below), which no onboard or tactile signal provides.

  3. Probing teacher, first version.

    The beam's geometry is hidden until a loaded foot has a beam edge under its taxels (|edge margin| < 35 mm, half the taxel span). This pushes the teacher to find the beam by touch. But once revealed, the full pose stayed visible, and its actions still depended on the CoM offset (0.11).

  4. Touch teacher (fine-tuned from it).

    Gets only what a tactile sole could tell it. Per foot: lateral offset and edge margin, only while that foot is loaded and feels an edge. Heading and heights: after the first felt edge. Base and CoM lateral offsets: never. The critic keeps full ground truth. Beam inputs now barely move its actions (≤ 0.006). It crosses 100 mm in 88% of runs but rarely 70 mm, and never 50 mm.

  5. Two students from the touch teacher.

    Identical GRU students and training, one with the 66 tactile features and one without.

Setup

Who sees what

Every observation term is tagged onboard (checked against the unitree_sdk2 LowState_: IMU and motor states, no base linear velocity, contact or terrain), sensor (the tactile slipper) or privileged. Registering a student task fails if any of its inputs is privileged, and a test checks that guard with deliberate leaks. Onboard and tactile inputs to the actors carry noise, biases and sensor faults; the critic's privileged copies are clean.

Teachers (PPO)

Actor and critic MLP 512-256-128, ELU, running normalisation, Gaussian with one scalar std. 4096 envs, 24-step rollouts, 5 epochs × 4 minibatches, lr 1e-3 adaptive (KL 0.01), γ 0.99, λ 0.95, entropy 0.005. Left-right symmetry augmentation and mirror loss (the mirrored state is checked against the G1's kinematics in a test).

Students (GRU)

GRU(256) → MLP 256-128, ELU, over the student's inputs: 94 dims blind, 160 with tactile. 50-step rollouts (1 s), truncated BPTT over 10 steps (the hidden state still carries the whole episode), 4 epochs: 20 gradient steps per iteration, lr 5e-4.

DAgger

The loss is MSE onto the frozen touch teacher's mean action at every visited state, weighted per joint by the action scale squared (i.e. joint-target error). The teacher drives for the first 200 iterations. The students then take over: at each step and in each env the teacher's action is executed with probability β, and β falls linearly to 0 at iteration 1500. 3000 iterations total, 4096 envs, starting on the widest beam.

Tactile features

The existing taxel pipeline is reused unchanged: per-contact forces give net force and CoP, four rays per taxel find which patches have support, and a Winkler plane splits the normal force over the 26 supported taxels. On top of that, each foot gets 33 numbers (66 for both). The right foot is re-indexed into the left foot's mirror image, so +y means lateral on both feet. Shear is left out: in this model it is the net tangential force split in proportion to load, so it adds no spatial information.

Reward

Reward terms (both teachers)

Both teachers use the same reward; the touch teacher differs only in what its actor observes. The reward is built from the walking reward, keeping its regularisers. Its foot-clearance and swing-height terms are dropped, because their raycasts hit the ground beside a narrow beam. Air time is relaxed. Lateral and yaw velocity tracking are gone with their commands. Weights are multiplied by the 0.02 s step. Terms that use the beam pose are computed from ground truth inside the reward, so the touch teacher is still rewarded for staying centred even though its actor cannot see the CoM offset.

Curriculum

Per-env width levels

LevelWidthSpawnCentreline σPushesTactile fault strength
  • Promote one level when the episode ends without an early termination and the robot either reached the end platform or covered at least 70% of the commanded distance. A commanded distance under 0.5 m counts as standing and only has to survive.
  • Demote one level on any early termination. A time-out without tracking keeps the level.
  • Everyone starts at 0.40 m. Spawns move to the start platform from 0.20 m, because the default stance is 0.31 m wide at the outer sole edges.
  • The share of standing commands falls from 60% to 15% as the mean level rises. Pushes start at 0.15 m and grow to ±0.3 m/s at the narrowest width.
  • Students follow the same rules on their own performance. While the teacher still drives, the levels track the teacher.

Domain randomisation: foot–terrain friction 0.4–1.2, contact softness (solref time constant 0.02–0.035 s), torso payload −1 to +3 kg, torso CoM ±2.5 cm, PD gains ±10%, effort limits 85–100%, actuation latency 0–20 ms, encoder bias ±0.015 rad, IMU noise plus a per-episode bias (gyro ±0.05 rad/s, gravity ±0.03), beam height 0.15–0.30 m, start offset ±0.08 m lateral and ±0.08 rad yaw, plus the tactile faults above.

Diagnosis

What each teacher's actions depend on

The teacher drives 512 envs spread over all widths. Every fifth step, one input at a time is replaced by its batch mean, and the change of the action is measured in joint-target space (mean squared change, weighted by each joint's action scale squared). A large value means the teacher relies on that input. A student that cannot observe the input must infer it from history, or it cannot copy the teacher.

Next

Next steps

  • Close the student gap with RL. Fine-tune each student with PPO, keeping the asymmetric privileged critic, starting from the DAgger weights. The students now walk on their own, so on-policy RL can correct what imitation leaves, at the widths where imitation alone fails.
  • Remove what the touch teacher still relies on that the students lack. Its actions depend about as much on the true base velocity (0.038) and its previous action (0.040) as on joint positions. Retrain or fine-tune the touch teacher without base linear velocity in its actor, and give the students their own previous action back once they drive (the copycat problem only exists while the teacher drives).
  • Narrow beams. The touch teacher itself stops at 70–100 mm. Without a CoM estimate it needs a better strategy: a slower speed at narrow levels, a more pronounced probing step, or explicit history (a GRU teacher) so it can integrate edge contacts over several steps.
  • Seeds and ablations. Everything here is one seed. Run 3–5 seeds per student before claiming the tactile margin. Also try the raw 156-dim taxel array in place of the features.
  • Curved beams. The beam is already a parametrised centreline and the symmetry mirror maps a left curve to a right curve. What is missing is a curved path generator, segment geometry, and a yaw-rate command.
  • Sim-to-real. Calibrate the tactile model (gain, noise, saturation, latency, crosstalk) against the real slipper, and replay recorded readings through the feature code. The rigid beam and the 7-capsule foot make edge contacts sharper than a rubber sole on wood.
